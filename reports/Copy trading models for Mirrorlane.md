# Mirrorlane Should Sell Copy Execution, Not Vaults

Copy trading makes its money from execution, not from followers' profits. Binance and its exchange rivals give leaders a 10–30% high-water-mark profit share and keep the trading fees. On Hyperliquid, the only copy product with real revenue, Hyperdash, charges a **5 bps fee on copied fills** (1.5 bps on ordinary trades) and earned about **$409K in the 30 days to 21 October 2026**, while native pooled vaults have stalled on both Hyperliquid and Lighter. Lighter already gives Mirrorlane every piece the winning model needs: any account's fills and positions stream publicly without credentials (its stop, take-profit and TWAP orders do not, and its resting limit orders show only in a public order-book snapshot), an app-held API key can trade a user's dedicated sub-account but can only withdraw to the user's own wallet, and a permissionless integrator fee of up to 10 bps per fill is paid in USDC. The catch is that non-zero integrator fees only work once the follower leaves Lighter's zero-fee Standard tier for Plus (0.5 bps) or Premium, and the tier applies to the whole wallet. Copy trading on Lighter is still close to empty — 341 user-run pools hold **$4.26M** against the protocol's own **$95M** LLP, Dextrabot is the only live third-party copier found, and LighterFluid's site is down — which is both the opening and the warning. Mirrorlane should launch non-custodial mirroring into dedicated Lighter sub-accounts, charge about 5 bps on copied fills (5.5 bps all-in, against 9.5 bps on Hyperdash) and about 1 bp on manual terminal trades, sell a flat subscription to users who stay on Standard, and add opt-in leaders paid from fee revenue later. The shared-services design already discussed holds up; the research adds that the signer must enforce a transaction allowlist because Lighter keys have no trade-only scope, that one IP can watch up to 5,000 leaders, and that the EC2 host must sit outside Lighter's restricted jurisdictions. Because regulators read automatic copying as portfolio management (EU, UK) or as directing accounts (US), the copy feature needs geo-blocking from day one for the US, UK, Canada, EEA retail and Lighter's other excluded countries — and at Lighter's current scale copy revenue alone will be modest, so it belongs inside the terminal rather than as a standalone business.

## Three ways to follow a trader, and only mirroring is growing

Every copy product studied uses one of three architectures, and that choice fixes custody, legal exposure and who gets paid. In the **exchange-custody model**, the venue holds all funds and runs the copy engine beside its matching engine: a Binance leader trades a dedicated lead portfolio that Binance calls "a separate sub-account", each copier gets an isolated copy portfolio per leader, and the engine places a real order in it for every leader order ([Binance Signal Copy FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/detail/75592719ba1d471ea9558325c6e8e788); [Binance Copy Trading Rules](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/binance-copy-trading-rules-30f1e3a2835345e0b8ffc87f261a8256)). In the **pooled-vault model**, followers deposit into one account the leader trades and receive pro-rata shares; Hyperliquid vaults and Lighter public pools both work this way and pay the leader a cut of profit when a depositor withdraws ([Hyperliquid vault leaders](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-leaders); [Lighter Public Pools](https://docs.lighter.xyz/trading/public-pools)). In the **delegated-key mirroring model**, the follower keeps funds in their own account and authorises an app's trading key; the app watches public leader activity, places its own orders in the follower's account and charges per fill ([Hyperdash Copy Trading](https://docs.hyperdash.com/copy-trade); [Dextrabot](https://dextrabot.com/)).

| Model and example | Custody | Leader detection | Execution | Follower pays | Leader earns |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchange custody: Binance Futures | Exchange; isolated copy portfolio per leader | Inside the exchange | One IOC limit order per copier, capped by slippage; market exits | Normal trading fees plus profit share | 10% default, up to 30%, plus 10% of copier fees (capped) |
| Pooled vault: Hyperliquid | Shared vault; depositors hold shares | Not needed (leader trades the pool) | Single account | 10% of profit at withdrawal | Fixed 10%; must keep at least 5% of the vault |
| Pooled vault: Lighter public pool | Pool sub-account of the operator | Not needed | Single account | Operator fee, typically 10–30% of profit | Operator fee |
| Delegated key: Hyperdash (Hyperliquid) | Follower's account plus no-withdraw agent key, or app-created Turnkey wallet | Public fills and position changes, plus periodic sync | Taker orders per follower | 4.5 bps venue + 5 bps app per fill | Nothing; copied wallets never opted in |
| Telegram bot: pvp.trade (Hyperliquid) | Turnkey-secured app wallet | Opted-in pvp.trade users only | Per copy account | 2.5–4 bps base fee | 10% of follower profit on close |
| Delegated key: Dextrabot (Lighter and Hyperliquid) | Follower's own account | Monitors wallet activity | In the follower's account | 5.5 bps per copied trade | Nothing |

Momentum is with mirroring. Hyperliquid raised its vault creation fee to **10,000 USDC** and relabelled HyperCore vaults "legacy", and monthly vault creations fell from 220 in March 2026 to 2 in the first three weeks of October ([Hyperliquid vault leaders](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-leaders); [Hyperliquid vault list](https://stats-data.hyperliquid.xyz/Mainnet/vaults)). On Lighter, 341 user-run public pools hold **$4.26M** combined and 314 of them hold under $1,000 ([Lighter publicPoolsMetadata](https://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/api/v1/publicPoolsMetadata?index=281474976710655&limit=100&filter=all)). Meanwhile builder codes, the fee rail mirroring apps use on Hyperliquid, generated **$10.17M in the 30 days to 15 September 2026** on $24.08B of volume ([HL HUB via KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/insight/META/6aa947a57d10fa0007cdaa20)). The two models also feel different. A vault depositor owns a slice of the leader's account and inherits its drawdowns, lock-ups and forced closes on withdrawal; a mirror follower keeps their own account, sets their own leverage and stops and can quit at any time, but always fills after the leader as a taker. Pool leaders are paid automatically, whereas mirroring apps rarely pay leaders at all, because the accounts they copy are public wallets that never opted in.

## Binance industrialises copying with isolated portfolios and a 10–30% profit share

Becoming a Binance Futures lead trader is cheap; climbing the tiers is not. Anyone can open a public or private lead portfolio with **500 USDT** and no track record, and public portfolios start with 200 copier slots that grow in steps of 100 to 800, with 800–1,000 reserved for Legend-badge leaders ([How to Lead Trade on Binance Futures](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/how-to-lead-trade-on-binance-futures-6acfb4c1f50c4db1b9e915181ff31a4c)). Bots are allowed but labelled: a lead portfolio can carry two IP-whitelisted API keys limited to **20 orders per 10 seconds**, and such portfolios carry an "API" tag that copiers can filter out ([Binance lead portfolio API keys](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/2bec848b904b422197ce121d0925f20b)). The official Copy Trading API exposes only two read endpoints, so outsiders cannot build on Binance's copy graph ([binance-connector-js](https://github.com/binance/binance-connector-js/blob/master/clients/copy-trading/src/rest-api/rest-api.ts)). In July 2026 Binance added Smart Money Signal copy trading, which mirrors an invited leader's main Futures account instead of requiring a separate stake ([Signal Copy FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/detail/75592719ba1d471ea9558325c6e8e788)). Followers choose a fixed margin per trade (10–50,000 USDT) or a fixed ratio on 10–300,000 USDT, then set leverage (follow the leader or fixed 1x–10x), a maximum entry slippage of 0.1–3% that defaults to 0.5% for BTC and ETH and 1.5% elsewhere and applies to opens only, per-position TP/SL, a portfolio stop loss, whether to copy only new positions, and whether stopping closes everything or hands positions back ([How to Use Copy Trading on Binance Futures](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/how-to-use-copy-trading-on-binance-futures-0b3a91eea664402f812fe41358c8a206)).

The execution engine is the most reusable template in this research. A leader's taker order is copied immediately as an **IOC limit order** priced at the copier's slippage cap; a leader's maker order is copied only once **fully filled**; and every close goes out as a **market order** ([Binance Copy Trading Rules](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/binance-copy-trading-rules-30f1e3a2835345e0b8ffc87f261a8256)). Fixed-ratio sizing maps the leader's margin usage onto each copier's balance, partial closes are mirrored proportionally, a copy order is capped at 80% of the contract's market depth, combined leader-plus-copier notional is capped at min(max notional, 0.8/leverage × depth), and leverage on low-liquidity symbols is capped at 5x (same source; [How to Lead Trade](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/how-to-lead-trade-on-binance-futures-6acfb4c1f50c4db1b9e915181ff31a4c)). A copy portfolio that drops below the minimum copy amount and fails five consecutive copies is closed automatically ([Binance Futures Copy Trading FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/frequently-asked-questions-on-binance-futures-copy-trading-6ed0995daf0b42d5816beaf1e31ca09d)). Because maker orders are copied only after they fill, copiers always enter after the leader at a taker price — a gap built into the design that no exchange engine removes.

The money flows explain why Binance runs the product. The leader takes **10% of copier profit by default and up to 30%** as Growth Plan badges unlock, and badges require copier PnL, assets under management, run time and a drawdown ceiling: Cadet needs at least 50 USDT of 7-day copier PnL and 10,000 USDT of 30-day average AUM, Legend 10,000 USDT and 3M USDT ([Binance Lead Trader Growth Plan](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/binance-futures-copy-trading-lead-trader-growth-plan-3160c961b2734d1cbb5342d1e86c6cdb)). Since 23 September 2024 profit share settles weekly on **total PnL, unrealized included**, minus what has already been paid — a cumulative high-water mark with no clawbacks; in Binance's worked example the leader gets 20 USDT in week one, nothing in weeks two and three while losses are recovered, and 10 USDT in week four ([Binance Lead Trader Benefits](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/lead-trader-benefits-in-binance-futures-copy-trading-ea9bacf82b9e4ddfae50ebc98565241b)). Leaders also receive **10% of their copiers' trading fees**, capped at 0.3% of copier margin per week, which ties the rebate to AUM rather than churn (same source). No document shows Binance taking a cut of profit share; it earns the trading fees, net of the leader rebate and affiliate commissions of up to 50% ([Growth Plan](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/binance-futures-copy-trading-lead-trader-growth-plan-3160c961b2734d1cbb5342d1e86c6cdb)). For Binance, copy trading acquires volume; it is not a revenue line of its own.

Binance publishes no copy-trading traction figures — its 2025 review cites 300M users and 1.2M Smart Money subscribers and never mentions copy trading ([Binance 2025 Year in Review](https://public.bnbstatic.com/reports/2025_EOY_Report.pdf)). Its regulatory posture is restrictive: it withdrew copy trading from the EEA in June 2024 ahead of MiCA ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/mica-is-already-here-binance-restricts-eu-users-from-copy-trading-and-many-stablecoins/)), requires proof-of-address KYC for spot copying and says availability varies by country ([Binance Spot Copy Trading FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/frequently-asked-questions-on-binance-spot-copy-trading-1826c3b426a149949851554bdde227d3)). Its anti-gaming rules target presentation and capacity: imitators of popular portfolios are hidden, leaders who promise returns or steer copiers off-platform are taken offline, and a leader who closes a losing portfolio within 7 days of opening it gets a 72-hour cooling-off ([How to Lead Trade](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/how-to-lead-trade-on-binance-futures-6acfb4c1f50c4db1b9e915181ff31a4c)).

| | Binance Futures | Bitget Futures | Bybit (crypto) | OKX |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leader profit share | 10% default, up to 30% by badge | 0–10% (private mode up to 99%; CFDs up to 30%) | 10/10/12/15% by rank | Up to 30% by level |
| Settlement | Weekly, total PnL incl. unrealized, high-water mark | Daily, only when flat, new profit only | Weekly, deducted daily and refunded on net loss | Weekly, withheld daily and refunded at settlement |

The benchmarks run the same model with different caps ([Bitget Elite Trader guide](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603847588); [Bybit profit sharing](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Copy-Trading-Profit-Sharing-Explained); [OKX profit sharing rules](https://www.okx.com/help/lead-traders-trader-profit-sharing-rules)), and none documents a platform cut of profit share, so the industry anchor is about 10% with up to 30% for top or private leaders. Bitget rewrote its ROI formula in June 2026 after elite traders inflated ROI by depositing large sums briefly ([Bitget announcement](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605456280)), and as of October 2026 held no MiCA authorisation and prohibited Austria, France and Germany ([CASP Tracker](https://casptracker.eu/exchange/bitget/)). Bybit recruits leaders with prize pools, such as 300,000 USDT for a challenge requiring 20 active followers ([AAP/Cision](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260209ae83704)). BitMEX now copies Hyperliquid leaders onto its own books with a 15% profit share ([BitMEX press release](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/globenewswire1001163188/); [BitMEX fees](https://support.bitmex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/33677673599005-What-are-the-fees-to-copy-trade-Hyperliquid-Leaders)) — an exchange importing DEX leaders, the reverse of what Mirrorlane could do.

## Hyperliquid shows that builder fees, not vaults, pay for copy products

### Vaults offer fixed 10% economics and have stopped growing

Anyone can open a Hyperliquid vault with a 100 USDC deposit and, now, a **10,000 USDC creation fee**; the leader receives a fixed **10% of profits**, must keep at least **5%** of the vault, and cannot trade spot or HIP-3 markets from a legacy vault ([Hyperliquid vault leaders](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-leaders)). Depositors face a 1-day lock-up, and when a withdrawal needs more margin than the vault has free, the vault cancels orders and then closes 20% of positions at a time ([Hyperliquid vault depositors](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-depositors)). The docs now steer builders towards HyperEVM vaults with ERC-4626 accounting and authorised agents ([Hyperliquid Vaults](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults)). Of 9,478 vaults ever created, 3,081 are open, holding about **$94.9M** of user-vault TVL with the top 10 holding 64.6%, and several of the largest are bleeding — drkmttr lost $5.05M over 30 days ([Hyperliquid vault list](https://stats-data.hyperliquid.xyz/Mainnet/vaults)). The leader's 10% is hard-coded and cannot be split with a front-end, so vaults give a front-end nothing beyond ordinary trading fees.

### HLP shows what pooled risk costs depositors

The protocol's own vault, HLP, peaked at **$603.9M in September 2025 and held $177.8M on 21 October 2026**, while its yearly return fell from about 32% in 2024 to about 7% so far in 2026 ([Hyperliquid info API](https://api.hyperliquid.xyz/info)). Three 2025 incidents pushed toxic flow onto depositors. In March an ETH whale withdrew collateral from a leveraged long and let it liquidate, costing HLP about $4M and triggering more than $130M of withdrawals in a day ([Arkham](https://info.arkm.com/research/hyperliquid-whale-passes-4m-loss-to-hlp-vault)); the same month a JELLY squeeze ended with a validator vote to delist the market and a Foundation pledge to make users whole ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/311713)); and in November a POPCAT manipulation left about $4.9M of bad debt and briefly halted withdrawals ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-hyperliquid-hack-november-2025)). A pool spreads one actor's toxic flow across every depositor and invites bank runs; mirroring keeps each follower's losses inside their own account.

### Builder codes and agent keys created a third-party economy

Builder codes let any front-end add up to **10 bps on perp fills** (1% on spot). The user approves a maximum once with their main wallet — an agent key cannot do it — fees accrue on-chain, and each user can hold at most 10 active approvals ([Hyperliquid builder codes](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/builder-codes)). Phantom's builder address had accrued **$27.5M** by 21 October 2026 ([Hyperliquid info API](https://api.hyperliquid.xyz/info)). Pricing has split into two camps: retail wallets such as MetaMask and Trust Wallet charge the full 10 bps, while pro terminals such as Hyperdash and Insilico charge 1–1.5 bps and compete on volume ([OpenChainBench](https://openchainbench.com/benchmarks/hyperliquid-frontends)). Underneath sits the agent (API) wallet, which "can place trades on behalf of the account" but "does not have permission to transfer or withdraw funds" ([Hyperliquid Python SDK](https://github.com/hyperliquid-dex/hyperliquid-python-sdk/blob/master/examples/basic_agent.py)); an account can hold one unnamed and three named agents, plus two per sub-account, each valid for at most 180 days ([Hyperliquid exchange endpoint](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/exchange-endpoint)).

Revenue figures come from DefiLlama ([DefiLlama builder revenue](https://api.llama.fi/overview/fees/Hyperliquid%20L1?dataType=dailyRevenue)). Hyperdash publishes the most complete spec: followers copy or countertrade up to 10 traders, cap leverage between 1x and 40x, set a "max worse entry" of 0–50% against the leader's price, copy only newly opened positions, and whitelist or blacklist assets; each wallet logs every action including skipped trades and the reason, and stopping a copy closes every position in that wallet ([Hyperdash Copy Trading](https://docs.hyperdash.com/copy-trade)). It prices copying as a premium execution product — a **0.05% builder fee on copied volume** against 1.5 bps on ordinary perp trades, with copy volume excluded from rebates ([Hyperdash fees](https://docs.hyperdash.com/fees-referrals-rewards.md)). pvp.trade is the main leader-paying exception: leaders opt in and earn "10% of the profits of their followers on closing a position" and nothing from countertraders ([pvp.trade Copytrading](https://docs.pvp.trade/en-copytrading)). Its builder code earned $8.01M over its lifetime but only $8.1K in the latest 30 days, after pvp.trade bought Hyperdash in November 2025 ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/pvptrade-acquires-hyperdash-unveils-enhanced-v2-version-37252)). The winning pattern is distribution plus a terminal or wallet, with copying as one feature: standalone copiers (HyperX, Dexly, Katoshi) make about $29K a month between them, Hyperbot set a 1 October 2026 end date for trading and copy trading, goodcryptoX announced its shutdown for 9 October 2026, and CopyLiquid was still selling a token presale before launch ([HypeChain](https://hypechain.app/best-hyperliquid-trading-bots/); [Cipher Intelligence](https://cipher-intelligence.io/blog/hyperliquid-copy-trading/)).

### Exits, not latency, decide copier results

Production copiers detect leader activity through WebSocket `userFills` or by diffing positions, run a reconciliation loop that flattens anything the leader no longer holds, size each copy by the ratio of follower to leader equity, and send IOC orders a few ticks through the book rather than plain market orders ([Cipher Intelligence](https://cipher-intelligence.io/blog/hyperliquid-copy-trading/)). Hyperliquid caps each IP at **10 unique users across user-specific WebSocket subscriptions** ([Hyperliquid rate limits](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/rate-limits-and-user-limits)), so watching many leaders takes a fleet of IPs or a self-hosted node. The decisive variable is not speed: a developer who ran a copy bot for months found copies earned "about half of the source wallet's edge, mostly lost on exits", that entry lag under a minute barely mattered, and that the fixes were to close only when the source is net flat, mirror partial exits proportionally, and use a wide catastrophe stop instead of tight stops ([Cipher Intelligence summary](https://cipher-intelligence.io/blog/hyperliquid-copy-trading/)). No drain or key leak at a Hyperliquid copy app surfaced; the residual risk is a stolen agent key used to trade accounts into losses, which Hyperdash addresses by encrypting the agent key in the user's browser before storing it ([Hyperdash API wallets and security](https://docs.hyperdash.com/agent-wallets-security.md)).

## Lighter exposes everything a copier needs but pays only on paid tiers

### Public pools are Lighter's native copy product, and demand is thin

A Lighter public pool is a sub-account of its operator: depositors mint shares priced at total asset value over total shares, the operator takes an "operator fee" on profits when depositors withdraw, and the operator must hold a minimum ownership share that caps outside deposits, with no lock-up ([Lighter Public Pools](https://docs.lighter.xyz/trading/public-pools)) beyond a **5-minute cooldown** ([Lighter systemConfig](https://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/api/v1/systemConfig)). The operator fee can only ever be lowered ([lighter-python pool example](https://github.com/elliottech/lighter-python/blob/main/examples/public-pool/public_pool_create_modify.py)). The API docs gate pool creation at **$10M of paid trading volume** with up to six pools per address ([Manage Public Pools](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/manage-public-pools-shares)), though the user docs still say operators are whitelisted, and whether profit share uses a high-water mark is undocumented. On 21 October 2026, 343 pools held about $99.6M, of which LLP held **$95.30M** and the 341 user pools **$4.26M**; only 8 user pools exceed $100K, 198 charge no fee, and fee-charging pools cluster at 10–30% ([Lighter publicPoolsMetadata](https://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/api/v1/publicPoolsMetadata?index=281474976710655&limit=100&filter=all)). The largest, "Edge & Hedge" at $1.71M with a 20% fee, once held $5.93M ([TradingStrategy.ai](https://tradingstrategy.ai/vaults/protocols/lighter)). LLP itself peaked near $700M (same source) and is now open only to LIT stakers, at 1 LIT per 10 USDC deposited ([Lighter LIT utility](https://docs.lighter.xyz/about-lighter/lit-utility)); its collapse to $95M coincides with that gate, though no source states the cause.

### Public data allows mirroring of fills and positions; orders are only partly visible

Lighter's integration guide names **copy trading** as the standard example of "server-side on behalf of users", where the user approves an app key and the app's backend signs trades ([Lighter: Choose your integration](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/choose-your-integration)). Live tests on 21 October 2026 against a large third-party account showed exactly which data any app can read without that account's credentials.

| Data on another account | Public without credentials? | Channel or endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Fills, with both counterparties, position size before the fill and a flip flag | Yes | WebSocket `account_all_trades`, `trade/{market}` |
| Positions, balances, funding, margin, leverage | Yes | WebSocket `account_all`, `account_all_positions`, `user_stats`; REST `account` |
| Daily PnL with inflows and outflows | Yes | REST `pnl` |
| Leaderboard by PnL, ROI, volume or equity | Yes | REST `pnlLeaderboard` |
| Resting limit orders in the book, each with its owner's account index | Yes, by snapshot (at most 250 a side per call; checked live in a follow-up on 21 Oct 2026) | REST `orderBookOrders` |
| An account's own order list, including TP/SL and TWAP orders | **No** (auth required); the public account snapshot shows only order counts | `account_all_orders`, `accountActiveOrders` |
| Pool orders, trades and depositor flows | Yes | `pool_data`, `transfer/history` |

Sources: [Lighter WebSocket reference](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/websocket-reference); [Lighter account data](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/account-data); [Lighter pnlLeaderboard](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/reference/pnlleaderboard).

Mirroring must therefore be driven by fills and positions: a leader's stop, take-profit and TWAP orders stay hidden, and resting limit orders appear only in order-book snapshots that must be polled (a correction made after the first draft, which said they were invisible until filled). A leader who opts in can hand over a **read-only token valid for up to 10 years** that reveals orders but cannot trade or withdraw ([Lighter API keys](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/api-keys)). REST `trades` for main accounts answered without auth in testing even though the docs say auth is required ([Lighter trades reference](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/reference/trades)), so production code should not rely on it. Watching is cheap: a connection carries **500 unique accounts and an IP 5,000**, with 200 client messages a minute and a keepalive at least every 2 minutes — five hundred times Hyperliquid's per-IP allowance — while REST defaults to 60 requests a minute and a free Builder account lifts reads to **240,000 weighted units a minute**, where an `account` snapshot weighs 300 ([Lighter rate limits](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/rate-limits)). From a restricted region such as the US, the normal WebSocket is refused with error 20558; `?readonly=true` still returns data, but trading from that IP is blocked ([Lighter WebSocket reference](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/websocket-reference)).

### API keys can trade and withdraw to the owner, with no trade-only scope

A Lighter API key is bound to one account index and registered with the user's L1 wallet signature. It can trade, cancel, modify orders, set leverage and margin, manage sub-accounts, make **same-owner transfers** and request **secure withdrawals**, which can only go to the L1 address that created the account; transfers to another owner, fast withdrawals and approving an integrator with fees also need the L1 signature, and key indexes 0–3 are reserved for Lighter's own apps ([Lighter API keys](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/api-keys)). Lighter's guidance to copy-trading backends is blunt: "an API key can trade and can request secure withdrawals" ([Choose your integration](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/choose-your-integration)). Transactions queue per key, each with its own nonce stream, and Lighter recommends one key per workload ([Lighter core concepts](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/core-concepts)); an app can create a user's account and register its key with a single wallet signature ([Create accounts programmatically](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/create-accounts-programmatically)); and sub-accounts are capped at 4 on Standard, 16 on Plus and 64 on Premium and cannot be deleted ([Lighter account types](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/account-types)). The custody story is good but not perfect: a stolen key cannot send funds to an attacker, but it can trade the account to zero.

### Integrator fees are the only real revenue rail, and they require Plus or Premium

Lighter's Partner Attribution is permissionless. Any account can collect up to **10 bps on perps** and 100 bps on spot, set per order and credited in USDC; the user signs `ApproveIntegrator` with maximum fees and an expiry (an L1 signature when the integrator belongs to another owner), can revoke at any time, and can approve at most four partners. The binding line is this: "Trades containing partner attribution attributes with non-zero integrator fees will require the client submitting them to be on a Plus or Premium/Advanced account type" ([Lighter Partner Attribution](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/partner-integration); [Partner Attribution docs](https://docs.lighter.xyz/integrations/partner-attribution)). The referral programme is no substitute: it pays 10–20% of the fees referred users pay on paid tiers — 0.05–0.1 bps on Plus — earns essentially nothing on Standard, and excludes trades carrying a non-zero integrator fee ([Lighter Referral Program](https://docs.lighter.xyz/referral-program)).

|---|---|---|---|---|

Sub-accounts share their L1 address's tier and a wallet can downgrade at most once every 24 hours (same sources), so moving a follower to Plus to charge them also puts their main account on 0.5 bps unless copying runs from a separate wallet. The current integrator market is small and dominated by distribution rather than copying: in the week to 21 October, 60 integrators routed **$478.5M** and earned **$142.0K**, a blended ~3 bps across 3,960 accounts, with Minara AI routing $193.6M, Telegram Wallet charging 9 bps, Insilico Terminal 1 bp, and no copy-trading app among the leaders ([Litscan integrators](https://litscan.io/integrators)). Dextrabot copies Hyperliquid and Lighter wallets for "0.055% on trade size" but does not appear in Litscan's list, and in November 2025 it was telling users Lighter copying was free ([Dextrabot](https://dextrabot.com/); [Dextrabot on X](https://x.com/dextrabot/status/1989409860698657039)). LighterFluid marketed free copy trading; its site now returns a 404 and Litscan shows zero 7-day volume ([Litscan](https://litscan.io/integrators)). In both cases, free did not last.

### A top-four venue that has cooled since its points era

On 21 October 2026 Lighter ranked **#4 by open interest at $786.8M**, far behind Hyperliquid's $7.75B, with 24-hour volume of $3.04B by Castle Labs' count ([Castle Labs](https://castlelabs.io/research/perp-dex-rankings)) and $1.90B by Lighter's own API ([Lighter exchangeStats](https://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/api/v1/exchangeStats)). TVL was $819.6M against a $1.46B peak in December 2025, and 30-day fees were $6.03M against $18.1M in November 2025 ([DefiLlama Lighter](https://defillama.com/protocol/lighter)). The venue went offline for about 4.5 hours during the October 2025 liquidation cascade, when LLP lost 5.35% in a day ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/lighter-to-compensate-llp-holders-after-535-loss-25866)). Its terms exclude the US, Canada, the UK, China, Russia, Ukraine and sanctioned states, and reserve the right to suspend accounts for "Unauthorized access to the API" or "Impersonation of the frontend interface" ([Lighter Terms](https://lighter.xyz/terms)).

## Most followers underperform, and regulators treat auto-copy as asset management

### The evidence on follower outcomes is consistently poor

The largest crypto dataset is a vendor study — from a firm selling a competing managed product — of 100,236 copier outcomes on Binance, Bybit and MEXC: **97.04% of leaders were profitable on their own books, but only 43.61% made money for their copiers**, and only 48.48% of copier outcomes were positive ([YieldFund](https://www.yieldfund.com/news/is-copy-trading-profitable-a-90-day-multi-exchange-study)). Hyperdash's own research on about 400,000 retail wallets using Hyperliquid apps found fees were "slightly more than half" of the $126M lost since November 2025, and only about 1 in 7 new traders was still active after three months ([KuCoin summary of Hyperdash research](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/hyperliquid-retail-traders-280-million-losses)). Academic work points the same way: lab experiments show that offering a copy option raises risk-taking, and on real platforms the top 5% of leaders account for 61–93% of copy relationships ([Apesteguia et al.](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/25396/1/copy%20trading%20experiment_32ms%20final.pdf)); a CHI 2024 study of crypto perp copy platforms found rankings "easily gameable" and "poorly correlated with future returns", documenting a leader showing +12% on an open portfolio just after closing another at −92% ([Kawai et al., CHI 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf)). Beyond weak returns sit outright abuses: pooled vaults absorbed whale-engineered losses on Hyperliquid, and custodial API keys failed catastrophically at 3Commas in 2022, where 44 users lost $14.8M before the company admitted the leak and about 10,000 keys were published ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/3commas-denies-accusations-of-leaking-api-data-resulting-in-14-8m-in-unauthorized-trades/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/118094/after-repeated-denials-3commas-admits-it-was-source-for-earlier-hacks)).

### Regulators look through "tool" labels to who decides the trade

In the EU, ESMA classifies automatic copy trading with no client action per trade as **portfolio management**, and says "auto-copy" buttons, approval windows and client-set leverage or stop-losses do not change that ([ESMA supervisory briefing, 2023](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)); the same reasoning was extended to MiCA services in April 2025 ([ESMA Q&A 2463](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ESMAQA2463.pdf)). On 24 February 2026 ESMA added that crypto perpetuals are "likely" CFDs under national product-intervention rules — leverage limits, negative-balance protection, a ban on incentives and a narrow target market — and called the product's commercial name "irrelevant" ([ESMA perps statement](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Dichiarazione-ESMA-24-febbraio-2026-ESMA35-243228190-8024.pdf)); commentators read that as a 2:1 retail leverage cap ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/regulation/esma-tells-firms-perpetual-futures-fall-under-eu-cfd-rules/)). Custody plays no part in the test. The UK's FCA likewise treats auto-copy as portfolio management even when the client sets amounts ([FCA copy trading](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/copy-trading)) and has banned retail crypto derivatives since January 2021 ([FCA PS20/10](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps20-10-prohibiting-sale-retail-clients-investment-products-reference-cryptoassets)).

In the US, causing trades without the client's specific authorisation is "directing" accounts — commodity trading advisor territory that removes the main exemption ([eCFR §4.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-4/subpart-A/section-4.10); [eCFR §4.14](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-4/subpart-A/section-4.14)). The CFTC held the 0x front-end liable in 2023 for leveraged products reachable through its interface and found Opyn's IP blocking "not sufficient" ([CFTC 8774-23](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8774-23)), and the 2026 wallet-interface reliefs from the CFTC and SEC explicitly exclude signal generation, discretion and order routing — exactly what a copy engine does ([Manatt on CFTC NAL 26-09](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/the-cftc-just-drew-a-line-between-wallets-and-brokers-here-s-what-it-actually-says); [Dechert on the SEC statement](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2026/4/sec-staff-provides-relief-for-crypto-wallet-interfaces.html)). Asia and the Gulf offer no easy home either: Singapore bans leveraged crypto trading for retail ([Rahmat Lim](https://rahmatlim.com/publication/articles/29214/mas-issues-revised-guidelines-on-provision-of-consumer-protection-safeguards-by-digital-payment-token-service-providers)) and since June 2025 requires a licence even for Singapore entities serving only overseas clients ([Blockhead](https://www.blockhead.co/2025/06/02/mas-sets-hard-june-30-deadline-for-unlicensed-crypto-firms-serving-overseas-clients/)), Hong Kong allows perps for professional investors only ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/hong-kong-permit-va-exchanges-offer-perpetual-contracts-and-engage)), and Dubai treats order-transmitting copy platforms as Broker-Dealers ([NeoLegal](https://neolegal.ae/insights/do-i-need-a-vara-licence)). eToro US shows what credible "tool" positioning takes — no leader compensation, no recommendations, no copy-specific fee, and a registered broker doing execution ([eToro CopyTrader disclosure](https://www.etoro.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Lets-Talk-About-CopyTrader-disclosure-October-2025.pdf)) — a position a paid, leader-ranked DEX copy engine cannot claim.

## Mirrorlane should charge about 5 bps per copied fill and pay leaders from fees

### Sell copy trading as a Plus-tier bundle priced per fill

The only on-chain rail that pays meaningfully on Lighter is the integrator fee, and it requires a paid tier, so Mirrorlane should sell "Plus plus copy" as one product. During onboarding each follower signs once to create a dedicated copy wallet or sub-account, register Mirrorlane's key on it, approve Mirrorlane as an integrator, and switch that wallet to Plus; a separate copy wallet keeps the user's main Standard wallet at zero fees, the same pattern as Hyperdash's "Create New" copy wallet and pvp.trade's per-copy accounts ([Hyperdash Copy Trading](https://docs.hyperdash.com/copy-trade); [pvp.trade Copytrading](https://docs.pvp.trade/en-copytrading)). Plus beats Premium for followers: Premium cuts the taker delay from 300 ms to 140 ms but charges 2.8 bps per taker fill instead of 0.5 bps, and the evidence says entry lag under a minute barely moves copier results. Mirrorlane should charge **5 bps per copied fill** and about **1 bp on manual terminal trades** (matching Insilico's 1 bp on Lighter), and exclude copy volume from any cashback as Hyperdash does; because Lighter's partner fee is set per order, copy and manual trades can be priced differently ([Lighter Partner Attribution](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/partner-integration)).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mirrorlane copy on Lighter Plus (recommended) | 0.5 bps | 5 bps | 5.5 bps | 11 bps |
| Mirrorlane copy on Lighter Premium | 2.8 bps | 5 bps | 7.8 bps | 15.6 bps |
| Dextrabot on Lighter | Not disclosed | 5.5 bps | ≥5.5 bps | ≥11 bps |
| Hyperdash copy on Hyperliquid | 4.5 bps | 5 bps | 9.5 bps | 19 bps |
| MetaMask or Trust Wallet on Hyperliquid | 4.5 bps | 10 bps | 14.5 bps | 29 bps |

Sources: [Lighter account types](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/account-types); [Hyperliquid fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees); [Hyperdash Copy Trading](https://docs.hyperdash.com/copy-trade); [OpenChainBench](https://openchainbench.com/benchmarks/hyperliquid-frontends); [Dextrabot](https://dextrabot.com/).

At 5.5 bps all-in, Mirrorlane's copy is **about 40% cheaper per fill than Hyperdash's** while still earning more per fill than Lighter's blended integrator average. Users who refuse to leave Standard can only be monetised off-chain, through a flat monthly subscription that bundles copy access with premium analytics, paid by card or a user-signed USDC transfer; Gainium sells $15–109 monthly plans with no builder fee as the alternative to its fee-based free mode, and HyperX lists a $199-a-year Pro tier ([Turbine](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/best-hyperliquid-trading-bots); [QuickNode listing](https://www.quicknode.com/builders-guide/tools/hyperx-by-hyperx)). Lighter's referral programme should be ignored as a revenue source.

### Unit economics depend on leader turnover

At 5 bps, each $1M of copied notional earns Mirrorlane $500. Lighter's entire integrator channel earned $142.0K on $478.5M in the latest week ([Litscan](https://litscan.io/integrators)); a Mirrorlane that reached **$50M of copied notional a week** — about a tenth of today's channel and a quarter of Minara AI's flow — would gross about **$25K a week, or $1.3M a year**. Per follower, the arithmetic exposes a trap. A $2,000 follower copying a swing trader who runs 2x leverage and turns the book over weekly generates about $35K of fills a month, worth about $17 to Mirrorlane, or 0.9% of the follower's equity. The same follower copying a 3x day trader who opens and closes daily generates about $360K of fills, worth about $180 — but that is **9% of equity a month** in fees, a drag no leader can overcome. Hyperdash's finding that fees were more than half of retail losses, and that most new traders churn within three months, is the same mechanism at scale ([KuCoin summary of Hyperdash research](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/hyperliquid-retail-traders-280-million-losses)). High-turnover leaders maximise this month's revenue and destroy next quarter's customers, so Mirrorlane should show each leader's expected monthly fee drag before a user copies them, keep minute-scale flippers out of default discovery, and test a monthly cap on copy fees as a share of follower equity — the same logic as Binance capping leader fee rebates at 0.3% of copier margin a week ([Binance Lead Trader Benefits](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/lead-trader-benefits-in-binance-futures-copy-trading-ea9bacf82b9e4ddfae50ebc98565241b)). Roughly 3,000 swing-profile followers would produce about $50K a month, close to the 3,960 accounts the whole Lighter integrator channel attributed in a week. Copy fees alone will not carry the company at Lighter's current scale; they must stack with terminal fees and subscriptions, and leader supply can be widened by copying Hyperliquid accounts onto Lighter, as Dextrabot does, accepting Hyperliquid's 10-users-per-IP limit on the watching side.

### Copy any account, but pay only leaders who opt in

Phase one should copy public Lighter accounts without paying leaders, exactly as Hyperliquid mirroring apps do, because Lighter's data model allows it without consent. Phase two adds verified leaders who prove ownership with a signature, share a read-only token so Mirrorlane can see their orders and every sub-account, meet minimum history and own-capital rules, and face Binance-style cooling-off after abandoning losing strategies. Mirrorlane would pay them a share of the copy fees their followers generate — 20% is a reasonable start against Binance's 10% — capped against follower margin and paid only while their followers' trailing copier PnL is positive, mirroring Binance's copier-PnL badge thresholds ([Growth Plan](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/binance-futures-copy-trading-lead-trader-growth-plan-3160c961b2734d1cbb5342d1e86c6cdb)) and answering ESMA's and IOSCO's concern that paying leaders per follower or per trade rewards churn ([ESMA briefing](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)). A profit share can come in phase three if demand appears, on Binance's template of 10% default with weekly high-water-mark settlement on total PnL; because a Lighter key cannot transfer value to another owner without the user's L1 signature ([Lighter API keys](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/api-keys)), settlement must be a user-signed transfer with copying paused until paid, never a silent sweep. Three things are worth refusing outright. Lighter public pools are a poor foundation, gated at $10M of paid volume, thinly demanded and carrying the heaviest legal exposure as a collective investment scheme in the EU and UK and a commodity pool in the US. Token presales are already read as a red flag. Leverage-linked fee discounts like pvp.trade's, up to −4 bps at 40x ([pvp.trade fee tiers](https://docs.pvp.trade/en-fee-tiers)), raise revenue by raising follower blow-up risk.

### Turn the backtest lab into the trust product

No Hyperliquid copy product publishes copier-realised performance, and that gap is Mirrorlane's best differentiator. The backtest-only lab can replay any Lighter leader's public fill history through Mirrorlane's exact copy rules — 300 ms taker delay, IOC slippage cap, minimum order sizes and 5.5 bps of fees — and show an expected copier return beside the leader's own. Lighter's public `pnl` endpoint includes inflows and outflows, so Mirrorlane can compute deposit-adjusted ROI from day one rather than retrofitting it as Bitget did after elite traders gamed ROI with deposit timing ([Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605456280)). Because every public Lighter trade carries both account IDs ([Lighter WebSocket reference](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/websocket-reference)), Mirrorlane can also detect a leader, or any sub-account under the leader's L1 address, taking the other side of follower flow. The AI strategy builder should stay out of the copy launch, and house AI strategies should not be published as copyable leaders, since that makes Mirrorlane the discretionary manager. Later, users can deploy their own AI-built rules on their own sub-accounts through the same signer and risk stack; the FCA exempts trade signals "fully determined by the client" from portfolio management ([FCA copy trading](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/copy-trading)), though whether AI-generated rules count as client-determined is untested.

### Launch as a geo-fenced software tool, not a manager

The copy feature should be blocked for everyone Lighter excludes — the US, Canada, the UK, China and sanctioned states ([Lighter Terms](https://lighter.xyz/terms)) — and also for EEA retail, from which Binance itself withdrew copy trading ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/mica-is-already-here-binance-restricts-eu-users-from-copy-trading-and-many-stablecoins/)), and for retail users in Singapore and Hong Kong. Blocking should combine IP checks with wallet attestation, VPN heuristics and sanctions screening, because the CFTC found IP blocking alone insufficient ([CFTC 8774-23](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8774-23)), and Mirrorlane should not incorporate in Singapore. Product rules matter as much as geography: no "copy the winners" marketing, leaders ranked on risk-adjusted full-history metrics that include abandoned strategies, the share of profitable followers shown openly, and follower counts and copied size capped against market depth. Using Lighter's documented copy-trading integration pattern, a free Builder account and distinct branding keeps Mirrorlane clear of the "unauthorized access to the API" and frontend-impersonation clauses. The research did not establish the best place to incorporate or the rules in Mirrorlane's remaining target markets such as Latin America, Turkey and Korea; that needs counsel before a public launch.

## The proposed shared-services design holds, with targeted Lighter changes

| Proposed component | Verdict | What the research changes or adds |
|---|---|---|
| One WebSocket watcher per leader | Confirmed, as subscriptions rather than processes | Public `account_all` / `account_all_trades` / `account_all_positions` need no auth; 500 accounts per connection and 5,000 per IP (2,500 leaders at two channels each); pace re-subscription after restarts under the 200-messages-a-minute limit; keepalive at least every 2 minutes; copying is fill- and position-driven because per-account order streams need auth and trigger orders are hidden |
| Redis Streams event bus | Confirmed | Carry `position_size_before` and the sign-change flag from each fill so workers classify open, add, reduce, close and flip without extra reads |
| Copy workers, one writer per follower | Confirmed, refined to one writer per API key | One nonce stream per key and one key per account index; run one copy sub-account per follower–leader pair (within Plus's 16), each with its own key and writer, for clean per-leader PnL and safe "stop closes everything" |
| Target-position copying plus reconciler | Confirmed and strengthened | Close only when the leader is net flat; mirror partial exits proportionally; IOC entries with a slippage cap (Binance defaults 0.5% BTC/ETH, 1.5% others); reduce-only exits never blocked by slippage; skip and log sub-minimum orders; copy new positions only by default; reconcile against public REST `account`; never depend on REST `trades` for main accounts |
| Isolated signer with KMS-encrypted keys | Confirmed; must become the policy engine | No trade-only scope exists, so sign only order create, cancel, modify and leverage changes; refuse withdrawals, transfers and key changes; enforce per-follower notional and leverage ceilings inside the signer |
| Per-follower risk guard and global kill switch | Confirmed; add venue-level guards | Cap aggregate follower notional per leader per market against book depth; exclude thin markets by default; trigger on stale feeds or venue outage; flag leaders who trade against copiers |
| Postgres for state and audit | Confirmed | Store every decision and skip reason, each order's integrator fee, follower high-water marks and copier-realised PnL per leader |
| Docker Compose on one EC2 | Adequate for the live test | Host outside Lighter's restricted list; single host is a single point of failure, so the reconciler must flatten stale positions on restart |
| Onboarding and fee plumbing | Missing from the design | Wallet signatures for account and key registration, `ApproveIntegrator` with fee cap and expiry, and the Plus switch; detect revoked approvals and replaced keys and pause the follower |

The signer change matters most. Lighter keys can request withdrawals to the owner's wallet and move funds between the owner's accounts, and no scope flag prevents it ([Lighter API keys](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/api-keys)); if KMS-held keys are signed by a generic signer, any compromised worker can trade every follower account into an attacker's resting orders, which is the realistic 3Commas-style failure on a venue where withdrawal theft is blocked. Allowlisting transaction types and enforcing notional limits inside the signer turns it from a vault into a policy boundary. Lighter's per-key nonce queues confirm the one-writer rule ([Lighter core concepts](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/core-concepts)), and binding keys to account indexes turns "per follower" into "per copy sub-account"; Plus followers get 4,000 transactions a minute plus a volume quota that starts at 1,000 transactions, ample for copying, though workers must still handle quota errors ([Lighter volume quota](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/volume-quota-program)). Hosting is not optional: from a US IP Lighter refuses the normal connection, so US-region watchers can only read via `?readonly=true` and the signer cannot trade at all ([Lighter WebSocket reference](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/websocket-reference)). Apply for the free Builder account before the test — at 240,000 weighted reads a minute and 300 per `account` snapshot, it supports about 800 position reconciliations a minute ([Lighter rate limits](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/rate-limits)). Lighter has gone dark for hours during a crash ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/lighter-exchange-outage-amid-19b-crypto-liquidation-28446)), so the kill switch must handle "venue unreachable" and not only "Mirrorlane misbehaving", and the reconciler's restart path should be proven by killing the host deliberately while positions are open.

### Run the live test in three gates

The first gate is shadow mode: watch 25–50 leaders drawn from `pnlLeaderboard` and filtered to accounts that traded recently and are not minute-flippers, compute target positions, and simulate fills against the live order book without sending orders. This measures what the research could not — the delay from a leader's fill to the WebSocket message, simulated slippage and the skip rate. The second gate uses house money: three to five Mirrorlane-owned wallets on Plus with a few hundred to $2,000 each, paying a real integrator fee to Mirrorlane's integrator account to validate `ApproveIntegrator`, the per-order fee fields and `partnerStats` reporting ([Lighter Partner Attribution](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/partner-integration)), with the host killed on purpose mid-position. The third gate is a closed beta for invited users from permitted jurisdictions, with allocation and leverage capped (5x echoes HyperX's default and Binance's thin-market cap) and copier-realised PnL shown from day one. The pass mark should be fixed before the first gate and should not be volume: it is the copier's captured share of leader edge net of fees (the developer benchmark is about half), zero signer-policy violations, and reconciliation drift that stays bounded and self-heals.

## Conclusion

On Lighter, the hard constraint for copy trading is monetisation and reach, not technology. The data, keys and fee rails all exist and Lighter documents copy trading as a supported pattern, but the integrator-fee rule turns every paying follower into a paid-tier customer, and Lighter's terms together with regulators' portfolio-management reading shrink the addressable market to a subset of non-Western retail. Copy trading's best role for Mirrorlane is therefore a premium execution feature that justifies the Plus upgrade inside a terminal people already use — the structure of the only profitable Hyperliquid copier — rather than a business of its own.

The more durable advantage is trust, and Lighter's design makes it unusually cheap to build. Every public fill names both counterparties and every account's flows are visible, so Mirrorlane can publish what no competitor does: copier-realised returns, deposit-adjusted leader ROI, expected fee drag, and evidence of leaders trading against their followers. Per-fill revenue and follower survival pull in opposite directions, and the products that last will be the ones that price and curate for the second; the live test should be judged on copier-captured edge, not volume.
